Mortgage life insurance vs term life

The insurance offered across the desk when you sign a mortgage is a different product from the one most people assume they are buying. With mortgage life insurance the lender is the beneficiary and the payout must go to the mortgage. The benefit shrinks as you pay the loan down, while the premium generally does not. And the Financial Consumer Agency of Canada is explicit that you do not have to buy it to get the mortgage.

The three differences that matter

Mortgage life insuranceTerm life insurance

How muchDeclines as the balance is repaid — and the premium is generally not reduced in step.Generally level for the whole term of the contract.
If you change lendersCoverage normally ends when the debt is transferred to another creditor. You reapply, at your then age and health.Stays in force. You own it, not the bank.

The FCAC's own conclusion on this comparison is that term or permanent life insurance may offer better value than optional mortgage life insurance. It also states plainly that you do not need it to be approved, that the lender cannot insist, and that buying it requires your express consent.

Why the declining benefit is the real cost

Picture a $600,000 mortgage. In year one, mortgage life insurance covers roughly $600,000. In year eighteen, with $200,000 left, it covers roughly $200,000 — and you have most likely been paying a similar premium throughout. You bought a shrinking asset at a flat price.

A level term policy behaves the other way round. The amount stays put while the mortgage falls, and the difference is not wasted: it is what pays for the childcare, the lost income and the time off work that a family actually needs in the first year after a death. The mortgage is the biggest number on the list, but it has never been the only one.

There is a second effect people miss. A term policy you own is portable. Switch lenders at renewal, move house, or refinance, and it carries on untouched. Creditor coverage attached to the loan generally does not, and reapplying five years later means doing it at an older age and with whatever health you have by then.

The questions to ask at the signing table

Mortgage closings are rushed and the insurance is often a one-page tick box. Four questions, and you want the answers in writing, before you sign:

  1. "Is my eligibility assessed now, or when a claim is made?" This is the single most important question and it is rarely volunteered. Ask for the certificate of insurance and read the definitions and exclusions, not the brochure.
  2. "What is the premium per $1,000 of coverage?" That is the only figure that lets you compare it with a term quote on the same amount. Without it you are comparing a monthly number against nothing.
  3. "What happens to this coverage if I switch lenders at renewal?"
  4. "Does the premium reduce as the balance falls?" If the answer is no, you now know what the later years cost you.

You can say "I'll take the mortgage, not the insurance" and arrange coverage separately. That is not an awkward thing to ask — the FCAC says your express consent is required, which means declining is a normal outcome, not an obstacle. If it was presented as a condition of approval, it was not one.

When the bank's version is genuinely the right answer

This is a comparison, not a warning, and there are real cases where creditor insurance is the better outcome:

  • Your health makes individual underwriting hard. If a term application would be declined, heavily rated, or take months, coverage attached to the mortgage may be the coverage you can actually get. Some coverage beats none, and a house is not a good thing to lose twice.
  • You need something in force this week. A closing date does not wait for underwriting. Creditor coverage can be a reasonable bridge while a term application is assessed — then you cancel it once the term policy issues.
  • The amount is small and the term is short. On a modest balance with a few years left, the difference may not be worth the paperwork.

What should not happen is accepting it by default at a closing because it was the only option on the table. There were two.

Getting the amount right

If you only insure the mortgage, you have insured the building. A fuller calculation generally covers the mortgage balance, other debts, the cost of raising any children to independence, and some replacement of lost income — less what you already have in coverage and savings.

That arithmetic, with Ontario examples, is set out in how much life insurance do I need. If you are also weighing policy types, term vs whole life covers that choice. And because a serious illness is statistically more likely than a death during the mortgage years, it is worth knowing what critical illness insurance does alongside it — a diagnosis that stops your income does not pay the mortgage either.

Frequently asked questions

Do I have to buy the bank's mortgage insurance to get a mortgage?

No. The Financial Consumer Agency of Canada states that you do not need optional mortgage insurance to be approved for a mortgage, that the lender cannot insist you buy it, and that it requires your express consent. If you felt it was presented as a condition of approval, it was not one. You can decline at the signing table and arrange your own coverage instead.

What is the real difference between mortgage life insurance and term life?

Three things. Who gets paid: with mortgage life insurance the lender is the beneficiary and the payout must go toward the mortgage, while a term policy pays a beneficiary you name, who can use it for the mortgage or anything else. How much: mortgage coverage shrinks as the loan is repaid, while a term policy's amount generally stays level for the whole term. Portability: creditor coverage normally ends when the debt moves to another lender, whereas a term policy you own follows you.

Does the premium go down as my mortgage balance falls?

Generally not. The amount the insurer could pay decreases as the balance decreases, but the premium is typically not reduced in step. That is the central criticism of the product: in the later years of a mortgage you can be paying a similar amount for materially less coverage. A level term policy priced at the outset does not behave that way.

Is mortgage life insurance ever the right choice?

Sometimes. If your health means individually underwritten life insurance is declined, rated heavily or very slow to issue, creditor insurance attached to the mortgage can be the coverage you can actually get, and some coverage beats none. It can also be reasonable as a short-term bridge while a term application is underwritten. Those are real situations, which is why this is a comparison rather than a warning.

What should I ask before accepting mortgage insurance at the signing table?

Ask for the certificate of insurance in writing before you sign, and read the definitions and exclusions. Ask specifically whether your eligibility is assessed now, when you apply, or at the time of a claim, and get that answer in writing. Ask what happens to the coverage if you switch lenders at renewal, and what the premium is per thousand dollars of coverage so you can compare it with a term quote on the same amount.

How much life insurance should I have if I have a mortgage?

The mortgage is usually the largest single number but rarely the only one. A fuller calculation covers the mortgage balance, other debts, the cost of raising any children to independence, and some replacement of lost income, less existing coverage and savings. A policy sized to the mortgage alone often leaves a family technically housed but short of money, which is why the amount is worth working out properly before shopping.
