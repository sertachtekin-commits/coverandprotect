International student health insurance in Ontario: UHIP and the gaps

UHIP costs $948 for the 2026-27 year, up from $792 — an increase of about 20% effective 1 September 2026. It is mandatory at Ontario's publicly funded universities and it covers hospital and physician care up to $1,000,000 a policy year. What catches students out is what it is not: it is not OHIP, and it does not cover prescriptions, dental or vision. It also does not cover your family until you enrol them.

What UHIP is, in one paragraph

The University Health Insurance Plan is a private group plan, not a provincial one. International students at Ontario's publicly funded universities are generally required to hold it, and it is normally billed to your student account rather than invoiced separately, which is why a lot of students do not realise they are paying for it until they look at a fee statement.

Colleges are a different question. UHIP membership is made up of Ontario's publicly funded universities. Colleges and private institutions generally arrange their own mandatory plans through other insurers, with different exclusions. If you are at a college, check your international student office rather than assuming this page describes your plan.

UHIP is not OHIP, and the difference costs money

UHIP is often described as "OHIP-equivalent", and for hospital and physician care that is roughly fair. But international students are generally not eligible for OHIP, so UHIP is the floor, not a supplement to something bigger. These are the four gaps:

Not coveredWhat it costs you
Prescription drugsAntibiotics, inhalers, ongoing medication — paid at the counter.
Dental careA single filling typically costs more than a year of supplementary coverage. A cleaning is not covered either.
Eye exams and glassesBoth, for most students.
Paramedical carePhysiotherapy after a sports injury, psychology, chiropractic, massage.

Most student unions sell a supplementary health and dental plan precisely to fill these, and for a single student living in residence it is often enough. The question worth asking is not whether you need something beyond UHIP, but whether the student plan's annual maximums cover what you actually use — they are usually modest, and dental maximums in particular run out quickly if any real work is needed.

If you are not covered by a student union plan, or you are comparing it against something broader, an individual health and dental plan is the alternative. We can price both side by side.

Your family is not covered until you enrol them

This is the single most expensive misunderstanding on this page. If your spouse or children live with you in Ontario, they must be enrolled in UHIP, and enrolment for dependants is a manual step. It does not happen when you register for classes, and each dependant carries their own premium.

Families arrive assuming the student's coverage extends to them. It does not until the paperwork is done. A child's emergency room visit in that window is billed in full, and the bill does not care that you intended to enrol. Do the dependant enrolment in your first week, not your first month.

Dependants also face the same four gaps above — drugs, dental, vision, paramedical — and for children, dental is the one that actually gets used.

When your parents come to visit

Your UHIP covers you, and your enrolled dependants if you enrolled them. It covers nobody else. Parents and grandparents flying in for a graduation, a semester, or a long stay need their own coverage, and they are never eligible for OHIP at any point in their stay.

  • A normal visit — visitor to Canada insurance, bought before they fly where possible. An uninsured hospital admission in Ontario runs into five or six figures.
  • A Super Visa — private emergency medical coverage of at least $100,000, valid for one year, is a condition of the visa itself. See Super Visa insurance.
  • If a parent has any health history — this needs a conversation before anything is bought. Insurers apply a stability period to pre-existing conditions, and a medication change in the weeks before the flight is the most common reason a large claim is refused. Call before you buy, not after.

A checklist for your first month

  1. Confirm on your fee statement that UHIP is there and paid — do not assume.
  2. Enrol any dependants living with you in Ontario. Manual step, first week.
  3. Find out what your student union plan covers and what its annual maximums are, especially for dental.
  4. Price an individual health and dental plan if there is no student plan, or to compare maximums.
  5. Before family visit, arrange visitor insurance — and if anyone has a health history, get it reviewed first.
  6. If you later become a permanent resident, the rules change: Ontario has no OHIP waiting period any more.

Frequently asked questions

How much does UHIP cost in 2026?

UHIP rose to $948 for twelve months of coverage in the 2026-27 year, up from $792, an increase of about 20 percent effective 1 September 2026. The new rate applies to returning students as well as new arrivals. It is normally billed to your student account rather than paid to an insurer directly, so check your fee statement rather than waiting for a separate invoice.

Is UHIP the same as OHIP?

No, and assuming it is causes real bills. UHIP is a private group plan that covers hospital and physician care similar to the core of OHIP, with coverage up to $1,000,000 per policy year. It is not a provincial plan and it does not cover prescription drugs, dental care or vision care. International students are generally not eligible for OHIP, so UHIP is the floor rather than a supplement to something larger.

Do international students need insurance on top of UHIP?

Many do, and most student unions sell a supplementary health and dental plan for exactly that reason. UHIP leaves out prescription drugs, dental care, eye examinations and glasses, and paramedical care such as physiotherapy or psychology. A single filling or a pair of glasses costs more than a year of supplementary coverage, so the question is usually whether the student union plan's maximums are enough rather than whether anything is needed.

Does UHIP cover my spouse and children?

Only if you enrol them, and enrolment for dependants is a manual step that does not happen automatically when you register. Students must enrol dependants who live with them in Ontario, each at their own premium. Families arrive assuming the student's coverage extends to them; it does not until the paperwork is done, and a child's emergency room visit in that window is billed in full.

Does UHIP apply to Ontario colleges as well as universities?

UHIP enrolment is tied to participating institutions, and the member list is made up of Ontario's publicly funded universities. Colleges and private institutions generally arrange their own mandatory plans through other insurers, and the terms differ. Check your own institution's international student office for which plan applies to you and what it covers, because the exclusions are not the same from plan to plan.

What insurance do my parents need when they visit me in Canada?

Visitor to Canada insurance, bought before or very soon after they arrive. Your UHIP or student plan covers you only. Visiting parents are not eligible for OHIP at any point in their stay, and an uninsured hospital admission runs into five or six figures. If they are coming on a Super Visa rather than a visitor visa, private emergency medical coverage of at least $100,000 valid for one year is a condition of the visa itself.
