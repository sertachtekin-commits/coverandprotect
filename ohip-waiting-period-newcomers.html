Is there still a three-month OHIP wait?

No. Ontario's official position is that there is no longer a waiting period for OHIP coverage, and that eligible people are covered as soon as their application is approved. A great deal of newcomer guidance still says three months, and it is out of date. What remains is a narrower gap — and a second one that never closes at all, which is the one most newcomers do not plan for.

What the province actually says

Ontario's own guidance on applying for OHIP states that there is no longer a waiting period, and that coverage begins once an eligible application is approved. The wait was suspended as part of the pandemic response and the province's current position is that none applies.

Removing the wait did not remove the eligibility rules, and this is where newcomers actually get caught. The three-month wait is gone; the residency and status tests are not.

To be eligible you generally need all three of the following:

  • To be physically present in Ontario for 153 days in any 12-month period, or for at least 153 of the first 183 days after you begin living in the province.
  • To have Ontario as your primary residence.
  • To meet at least one further condition on the province's list — being a Canadian citizen, a permanent resident, or a person registered under the Indian Act, among others. Some work permit holders qualify; visitors do not.

Every applicant needs their own immigration document from IRCC, children included. Because this list is the part that changes, check it on ontario.ca before you go, and take originals.

Why so much advice still says three months

The three-month wait was real for a long time, and it was removed relatively recently. Immigration blogs, settlement handouts and insurance marketing written before the change are still circulating, and some of them rank well. A few even state that the wait was reinstated, which contradicts the province's own page.

If you are reading a newcomer guide that still describes a three-month OHIP wait, it is out of date — and so is anything it tells you to buy on that basis. Check the date on the page, then check ontario.ca.

This matters commercially as well as factually: some newcomers are still being sold three months of private emergency coverage they may not need, while the gaps they genuinely will live with go uninsured.

The gap that does exist

Coverage starts on approval, not on arrival. So the honest exposure for a new permanent resident is the stretch between landing and completing a successful application: finding your documents, getting to a ServiceOntario centre, and having the application accepted. For an organised arrival that can be short. For a family arriving in winter with a lease to sort out and a school to register, it is not always.

Emergency medical insurance covering that interval is inexpensive, and it is worth having for the same reason anyone has it: a single ambulance ride and an overnight admission is a four-figure bill, and it does not wait for your paperwork.

Two groups should not treat this as a short gap at all. Visitors are never eligible for OHIP, however long they stay — see visitor to Canada insurance. Parents and grandparents on a Super Visa must hold private emergency medical coverage of at least $100,000 for a year as a condition of the visa itself — see Super Visa insurance.

The gap that never closes

This is the one worth your attention, and almost nothing written about the OHIP wait mentions it. OHIP covers physician and hospital care. It does not cover a long list of things people assume are included:

Prescription drugs taken at homeFor most adults between 25 and 64. Separate provincial programs cover many people under 25 and those 65 and over, but in between, a prescription is yours to pay unless you have a plan.
Routine dental careCleanings, fillings, crowns. A family of four's routine dental runs well into four figures a year.
Eye exams and glassesFor most adults between 20 and 64.
Paramedical carePhysiotherapy, massage, chiropractic, psychology — outside limited public programs.
Private or semi-private hospital roomsA ward bed is covered; a private room is billed.
Medical care outside CanadaOHIP pays a small fraction of a foreign hospital bill, which is why travel insurance exists separately.

Newcomers arriving without employer benefits — self-employed, contract, or still job-hunting — carry all of that personally from day one. An individual health and dental plan is what covers it, and unlike the OHIP question it is not a waiting-period problem that solves itself.

One timing point worth knowing. Individual health and dental plans are medically underwritten, and some have enrolment windows tied to losing previous coverage. Applying while you are healthy and recently arrived is generally easier than applying after a condition appears. This is the opposite of the OHIP situation: here, waiting costs you options.

If you are moving between provinces

Ontario has removed its waiting period. Several provinces, including British Columbia and Quebec, still apply one of roughly three months for new residents. If you land in one province and move to another, check both sets of rules — the gap belongs to whichever province you are in at the time, and interprovincial moves are where people fall between two systems.

Ontario residents who spend long stretches outside the province have the mirror-image problem: OHIP has day-count rules, and losing provincial coverage can also end a travel policy, because insurers generally define an eligible Canadian resident as someone enrolled in or eligible for a provincial plan. That is set out in the 212-day rule guide.

A short checklist for a new arrival

  1. Check the current eligibility list on ontario.ca and gather an immigration document for each person, children included.
  2. Apply as early as you can — coverage runs from approval, so every day of delay is a day of exposure.
  3. Cover the interval before approval with emergency medical insurance if anyone in the family has a health history or you cannot apply immediately.
  4. Separately, price an individual health and dental plan for the gaps OHIP never fills. Do this early, while underwriting is straightforward.
  5. If visiting relatives are coming, that is visitor insurance or Super Visa insurance — they are never eligible for OHIP.

Frequently asked questions

Is there still a 3-month waiting period for OHIP?

No. Ontario's official position is that there is no longer a waiting period for OHIP coverage, and that eligible people are covered as soon as their application is approved. A great deal of newcomer guidance still describes a three-month wait, which is out of date. You still have to be eligible and still have to apply: the wait is gone, the eligibility rules are not.

What are the OHIP eligibility rules for a new permanent resident?

Ontario requires that you be physically present in Ontario for 153 days in any 12-month period, or for at least 153 of the first 183 days after you begin living in the province, and that Ontario be your primary residence. You must also meet at least one further condition, such as being a Canadian citizen, a permanent resident, or a person registered under the Indian Act. Each applicant, children included, needs their own immigration document from IRCC. Confirm the current list on ontario.ca before you apply.

Do I need private health insurance when I arrive in Ontario?

Possibly, for a shorter period than the old three-month advice suggests. Coverage begins when your application is approved, not when you land, so the exposure is the stretch between arriving and completing a successful application. Emergency medical insurance for that interval is inexpensive. The larger and more permanent need is different: OHIP never covers prescription drugs for most working-age adults, dental, vision or paramedical care.

What does OHIP not cover, even once I have a card?

OHIP covers physician and hospital care. It does not cover prescription drugs taken at home for most adults between 25 and 64, routine dental care, eye examinations and glasses for most adults, physiotherapy, massage, chiropractic or psychology outside limited programs, or private and semi-private hospital rooms. It also pays only a small fraction of a hospital bill outside Canada. Those gaps are what individual health and dental insurance is for, and they do not close with time.

Are visitors and parents on a Super Visa covered by OHIP?

No. Visitors to Canada are not eligible for OHIP at any point in their stay, however long they remain. Parents and grandparents on a Super Visa must hold private emergency medical insurance of at least $100,000 valid for a year as a condition of the visa itself. Visitor to Canada insurance is a separate product from individual health insurance for residents.

Does every province work this way?

No, and this matters if you are moving between provinces. Ontario has removed its waiting period, but several provinces, including British Columbia and Quebec, still apply a waiting period of around three months for new residents. If you are landing in one province and moving to another, check the rules for both, because the gap belongs to whichever province you are in at the time.
