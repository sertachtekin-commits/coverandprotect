Super Visa Insurance Requirements in 2026: The Complete Checklist

By Sertac Tekin, licensed Ontario insurance advisor (FSRA Licence #10112782) · Reviewed November 2026 · 7 min read

The insurance requirement is the part of a Super Visa application that trips up the most families, and a refusal costs months. This is what IRCC requires in 2026, the documents that go with it, and the mistakes that cause delays.

The 2026 Super Visa insurance requirements

Every applicant must hold a qualifying medical insurance policy that meets all of the following:

  • At least $100,000 in coverage — emergency medical coverage of $100,000 or more. Higher limits are available and common, but $100,000 is the floor that satisfies the requirement.
  • Valid for at least one year from entry — a minimum of 365 days, starting from the applicant’s arrival date in Canada.
  • Covers the essentials — healthcare, hospitalization and repatriation (the cost of returning the person to their home country).
  • From a qualifying insurer — a Canadian insurer, or a foreign insurer meeting the OSFI conditions set out below.
  • Proof of paid coverage — evidence the policy is paid and in force, or approved, submitted with the application. A quote is not enough.

Can the policy come from outside Canada?

Sometimes — but the condition is narrower than most summaries suggest, and this is where the costly mistakes happen.

IRCC does not designate or approve individual insurers. There is no IRCC list to check. What matters is the insurer’s standing with Canada’s federal regulator. A policy from outside Canada qualifies only when the company is:

  • authorized by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) to provide accident and sickness insurance in Canada;
  • listed on OSFI’s register of federally regulated financial institutions; and
  • issuing the policy under its Canadian insurance business.

A broker, an agency or a claims administrator does not qualify, however Canadian the name sounds. We cover the checks and the proof officers expect in detail in Super Visa insurance from a foreign insurer: what qualifies.

In practice most families still choose a Canadian insurer, for a reason that has nothing to do with the application: if something goes wrong during the stay, claims and support are far simpler to handle from within Canada.

Documents you’ll need for the application

The insurance policy is one part of a larger application. Alongside qualifying coverage, IRCC expects:

  • Proof of Super Visa medical insurance — showing the $100,000 minimum, the one-year validity, and that it is paid or approved.
  • A letter of invitation from the child or grandchild in Canada, promising financial support and describing the household.
  • Proof the host meets the minimum income (LICO) — a notice of assessment, employment letter or equivalent, at or above the Low Income Cut-Off for their family size.
  • Proof of relationship — documents showing the applicant is the parent or grandparent of the host.
  • Proof the host is a Canadian citizen or permanent resident.

The LICO threshold rises with the host’s family size and IRCC updates the figures annually, so confirm the current number for your household before applying. That part belongs to the Canadian child or grandchild; the insurance is the piece an advisor handles.

How long is the coverage — and the visa?

A Super Visa can allow parents and grandparents to stay up to five years per entry, and the visa itself can be valid for up to ten years. The insurance only has to be valid one year at a time, so most families buy a one-year policy for the application and renew annually for as long as the stay continues.

If plans change, refund rules generally allow a refund when the visa is refused or the visitor returns home early — the terms differ by insurer, so check them before you buy rather than after.

Common mistakes to avoid

  • Buying too little coverage — under $100,000, or not valid for a full year, fails the requirement outright.
  • Submitting a quote instead of proof of purchase — IRCC wants evidence the policy is paid and in force, not a price estimate.
  • Assuming any foreign insurer counts — the OSFI authorization above is the test, and there is no IRCC list of approved companies to rely on.
  • Ignoring pre-existing conditions — if a parent manages diabetes or a heart condition, the policy should cover stable pre-existing conditions, or a related claim can be refused even though the policy satisfied IRCC. See how stability periods work.
  • Leaving it to the last minute — align the start date with the expected arrival date, and allow time to get proof of coverage before the deadline.

How much will it cost?

Premium turns mainly on the applicant’s age, the coverage amount, the deductible and whether pre-existing conditions are covered. Age is the largest single factor and the bands steepen after 70. Our Super Visa insurance cost guide breaks down 2026 pricing by age and the levers that actually lower a premium — a higher deductible being the most useful one.

Because the requirement is a floor rather than a product, two compliant policies at a similar price can differ sharply in what they do with a medical history. That difference is the whole value of comparing.

The bottom line

Super Visa insurance in 2026 must be at least $100,000, valid for a year from entry, cover healthcare, hospitalization and repatriation, and come from a Canadian insurer or an OSFI-authorized foreign one — with proof of payment in the application. Get those right and the insurance side is handled.

The part worth a conversation is not the checklist. It is the medical questionnaire, because that is what a claim gets checked against years later.

Frequently asked questions

What are the Super Visa insurance requirements in 2026?

The policy must provide at least $100,000 in emergency medical coverage, be valid for a minimum of one year from the date of entry, and cover healthcare, hospitalization and repatriation. It must come from a Canadian insurer, or from a foreign insurer that OSFI has authorized to provide accident and sickness insurance in Canada. Proof that the policy is paid and in force must be included with the application — a quote is not enough.

Can the policy come from an insurer outside Canada?

Only in a narrow case. IRCC does not designate or approve individual insurers. The foreign company must be authorized by the Office of the Superintendent of Financial Institutions (OSFI) to provide accident and sickness insurance, must appear on OSFI's list of federally regulated financial institutions, and the policy must be issued under its Canadian insurance business. A broker or a claims administrator does not qualify.

How long must Super Visa medical insurance be valid?

At least one year — 365 days — from the applicant's date of entry into Canada. The Super Visa itself can allow stays of up to five years per entry and remain valid for up to ten years, so most families buy one year for the application and renew annually while the parent or grandparent is in Canada.

Can Super Visa insurance be paid monthly?

Yes. Monthly payment plans are accepted as long as the insurer issues proof of a full year of coverage for the application. What IRCC looks at is the policy's term and that it is in force, not how the premium is scheduled.

Is a quote enough proof for the application?

No. IRCC expects evidence that the policy is paid and in force, or approved. A price estimate or an unpaid quote is one of the most common reasons an otherwise complete Super Visa application is delayed.

What if the parent has a pre-existing condition such as diabetes or a heart condition?

The policy should cover stable pre-existing conditions, or a related claim can be refused even though the policy satisfied IRCC. Each insurer sets its own stability period, so a history that misses one insurer's window can sit inside another's. Answer the medical questions exactly; an inaccurate answer is the most common reason a large claim is denied.

How much does Super Visa insurance cost in 2026?

Premium depends mainly on the applicant's age, the coverage amount, the deductible and whether pre-existing conditions are covered. Age is the largest single factor and premiums step up sharply after 70.
