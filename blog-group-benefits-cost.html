How Much Do Group Benefits Cost for a Small Business in Ontario?

By Sertac Tekin, licensed Ontario insurance advisor (FSRA Licence #10112782) · Reviewed November 2026 · 8 min read

For most small Ontario businesses in 2026, group benefits cost roughly $75 to $250 per employee per month — about $900 to $3,000 per employee per year. Where a plan lands depends on the team’s size, ages, industry, and how rich you make the coverage.

If you are weighing a benefits plan for the first time — a shop, an office, a trades crew, a growing startup — cost is usually the first hurdle. This is what 2026 pricing looks like by tier, what pushes it up or down, how the tax treatment works in your favour, and the legitimate ways to keep it affordable.

What makes the price go up or down

1. Group size

Very small groups of two to four people are priced more cautiously, because a single large claim has an outsized effect on the pool. As the team grows, pricing tends to stabilise and per-employee cost often improves.

2. Employee demographics

Average age and the mix of single versus family coverage are major drivers. A younger team costs less to insure, and employees electing family coverage cost more than single members.

3. Industry and occupation

Your line of work affects disability and life pricing in particular. Physically demanding trades — construction, manufacturing, transport — typically carry higher disability rates than desk-based work, because the risk of an injury that keeps someone off the job is higher.

4. Plan design

This is the lever you control most. Deductibles, co-insurance (the plan pays 80% and the employee 20%, say), annual maximums on paramedical and dental, and drug plan design all move the premium. A leaner design meaningfully lowers cost; a richer one raises it.

5. Claims history

Once a plan has been in force, its own claims experience feeds into renewal pricing. A year of heavy claims can push the next renewal up; a stable group helps keep it in check.

The tax advantage. Premiums your business pays for a group plan are generally a deductible business expense, and outside Quebec, employer-paid health and dental benefits are normally received by employees tax-free. A benefits dollar often goes further than the same dollar paid as taxable salary — which is much of why benefits work as a retention tool. Five ways to keep a small-business plan affordable

  • Share the premium — splitting cost with employees through payroll, commonly somewhere between 50/50 and 80/20, is the most direct way to fit the plan to a budget while still offering real value.
  • Start lean, then grow — launch Basic or Standard and add modules (disability, enhanced dental, critical illness) as the business grows.
  • Use co-insurance and sensible caps — 80% co-insurance and reasonable annual maximums trim premium without gutting the benefit.
  • Add a Health Spending Account — an HSA gives employees a fixed, tax-effective pool, capping your cost while keeping flexibility. It pairs well with a lean base plan.
  • Have it marketed properly — an independent advisor takes your group to every insurer we are appointed with, compares the quotes on the same basis, and structures the design so you are not paying for coverage your team will not use.

Is a group plan worth it for a small business?

For most owners, yes, for two reasons beyond the coverage itself. Benefits are one of the strongest tools for keeping good employees, especially in competitive trades and skilled roles where wages alone are not enough. And the tax treatment makes benefits a more efficient form of compensation than an equivalent raise. If there are even two or three people you want to keep, a modest plan is usually worth pricing out.

A group plan also complements the individual protection key people often need — critical illness coverage that pays a tax-free lump sum on a serious diagnosis is a common addition for owners and senior staff.

The bottom line

Small-business group benefits in Ontario typically run $75–$250 per employee per month, driven by group size, ages, industry and plan design — and softened by a business deduction and tax-free health benefits for staff. The only way to know your number is a quote built around your actual team.

Frequently asked questions

How much do group benefits cost per employee in Ontario?

For most small Ontario businesses in 2026, roughly $75 to $250 per employee per month — about $900 to $3,000 a year — depending on how comprehensive the plan is. A basic extended health and dental plan sits at the lower end; adding life, disability and enhanced caps moves it up. These are illustrative ranges for budgeting, not a quote.

What makes a group benefits plan more expensive?

Five things: group size (very small groups of two to four are priced more cautiously, because one large claim moves the whole pool), the average age and single-versus-family mix of the team, the industry (physically demanding trades carry higher disability rates than office work), the plan design itself, and — once the plan has run a year — its own claims experience at renewal.

Are group benefits tax deductible for a small business?

Can a business with only two or three employees get a group plan?

Yes. Plans are available for very small groups, though pricing is more cautious at that size because a single large claim has a bigger effect on the pool. Owners with two or three people they want to keep usually find a modest plan worth pricing out.

How can a small business lower the cost of a benefits plan?
